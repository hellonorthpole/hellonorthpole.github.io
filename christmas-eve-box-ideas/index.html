What to put in a Christmas Eve box

  • New pyjamas. The Christmas Eve classic, ready for the photos on Christmas morning.
  • A Christmas book. Read it together by the tree before bed.
  • Hot chocolate. A sachet, a mug and a few marshmallows.
  • Reindeer food. Oats and a few seeds to sprinkle outside. No glitter, so it's kind to birds.
  • Cosy socks or slippers. Warm toes for the big wait.
  • A torch. For spotting the sleigh from the window.
  • A film for the evening. Everyone on the sofa, under one blanket.
  • A note from the elves. Our favourite: "Listen out for the phone…"

Ideas by age

Toddlers: a board book, a soft toy, bath bubbles and pyjamas. Small and simple is perfect.

Ages 4 to 7: reindeer food, a torch, a letter from the elves and a hot chocolate.

Older children: a new card game, a hot chocolate kit and a film night they pick.

Questions parents ask

What do you put in a Christmas Eve box?

A typical Christmas Eve box in the UK has new pyjamas, a Christmas book and a little treat such as hot chocolate or sweets. Other favourites are reindeer food to sprinkle outside (oats and seeds, with no glitter, so it's safe for birds), cosy socks or slippers, a torch for spotting the sleigh, a Christmas film for the evening and a short note from the elves. You don't need all of them. Three or four well-chosen things feel just as special, especially if the box comes out at the same time every year.

What should go in a Christmas Eve box for a toddler?

For a toddler, keep it small, soft and simple: a board book with big pictures, a soft toy, new pyjamas and some bath bubbles for a calm wind-down. Skip anything with small parts. Little ones love repetition, so the same box and the same order of things each year (bath, pyjamas, story) quickly becomes a tradition. If you'd like Father Christmas to ring, book an early evening slot before the bedtime routine, and let them sit on your lap with the speaker on.

When do you open a Christmas Eve box?

It's usually opened on the afternoon or early evening of 24 December, often after tea or after bath time, so the pyjamas can go straight on. Some open it a little earlier so there's time for the film and the hot chocolate before bed. There's no rule: pick a time that suits your family and keep it the same each year so everyone looks forward to it. If you've booked a call from Father Christmas, opening the box just before the call is a lovely way to build up to the moment the phone rings.

How much should a Christmas Eve box cost?

As little or as much as you like. A Christmas Eve box is about the ritual, not the price. Reuse the same box every year, fill it with things you'd buy anyway, such as pyjamas and a book, and add one small treat. Plenty of the nicest ideas cost almost nothing: a note from the elves, homemade reindeer food from your kitchen cupboard, or a Christmas film you already own.
