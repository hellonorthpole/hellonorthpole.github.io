8 Christmas Eve traditions for toddlers

  1. Sprinkle reindeer food. Oats and a few seeds on the doorstep. No glitter, so it's kind to birds.
  2. Unwrap a Christmas Eve book. Read it by the tree, then keep it for next year.
  3. Bath, then new pyjamas. A calm routine, with a festive twist.
  4. Put out a mince pie and a carrot. Let them carry the carrot. It's a very important job.
  5. Sing one carol by the tree. Just one. The same one each year.
  6. Look for the sleigh. Lights off, torch on, noses to the window.
  7. Watch a short Christmas film. Cuddled up under one blanket.
  8. A call from Father Christmas. The phone rings at teatime, and the room goes quiet.

Questions parents ask

What can you do on Christmas Eve with a toddler?

Keep it simple and gentle. Toddlers love small, repeated rituals more than big plans, so pick three or four things and do them in the same order each year: sprinkle reindeer food outside (oats and seeds, no glitter), read one Christmas book by the tree, have a bath and put on new pyjamas, and leave out a mince pie and a carrot. A short Christmas film, one carol by the tree or a torch-lit look for the sleigh at the window all work well too. If you'd like a magical moment the whole family shares, Father Christmas can ring your phone at teatime.

Is a call from Father Christmas OK for a toddler?

Yes, as long as you keep it cosy. Sit together with your little one on your lap and the speaker on, so they can hear him with you right there. Father Christmas is always kind and gentle, never scary, and he's happy to do most of the talking, so they don't need to say much at all. You can tell us how to say their name, one or two proud moments and anything to avoid when you book. The call lasts about five minutes, and you're holding the phone the whole time.

What if my toddler is shy or gets upset?

That's completely normal, and you're in charge. Father Christmas speaks gently, and he won't push for answers. If your toddler would rather just listen, that's lovely. If they'd rather hide behind you, he'll happily chat with the rest of the family. Having everyone close by with the speaker on helps little ones feel safe. You can share anything to avoid when you book, and he'll steer clear of it.
